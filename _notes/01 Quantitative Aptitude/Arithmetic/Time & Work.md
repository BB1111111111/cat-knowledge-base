# Time & Work

## Concepts

### Work, workers and time

For equally efficient workers working at a constant rate, the amount of work completed is proportional to the number of workers and the time spent working. For a fixed job, workers and time are inversely proportional.

> [!abstract] Core idea
> For a fixed job with equally efficient workers, **workers × days = constant**.

## Formulas / rules

### Constant work

If work and efficiency per worker remain unchanged:

$$
W_1D_1=W_2D_2
$$

where W is the number of workers and D is the number of days.

The total work can be measured in worker-days:

$$
\text{Total work}=\text{Workers}\times\text{Days}
$$

This assumes equal efficiency, equal daily working hours and unchanged working conditions.

## Worked examples

### Trick 2. Constant work — changing the number of workers

**Question**

Suppose 10 workers finish a job in 12 days. How many days will 15 equally efficient workers take?

**Traditional method**

Total work:

$$
10\times12=120\text{ worker-days}
$$

Let the new time be T:

$$
15T=120
$$

$$
T=8\text{ days}
$$

> [!success] Answer
> **8 days.**

**Shortcut**

$$
W_1D_1=W_2D_2
$$

$$
10\times12=15\times T
$$

$$
T=\frac{120}{15}=8\text{ days}
$$

> [!tip] CAT shortcut
> When equally efficient workers increase in number, the time decreases inversely. Cross-multiply directly.

## Common traps

- Do not apply the inverse-proportion shortcut blindly when workers have different efficiencies.
- The total job must remain unchanged.
- Daily working hours and individual productivity must remain constant.

## Practice and mistakes

- [ ] I can calculate total work in worker-days.
- [ ] I can apply W₁D₁ = W₂D₂ to a fixed job.
- [ ] I check the equal-efficiency assumption.
